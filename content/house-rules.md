## Character Creation & Advancement

### Hopeless Losers

A truly hopeless character does not have to enter play as a player character.

> **A character counts as a hopeless loser when all six ability scores are below 9, or when two or more ability scores fall in the 3–6 range.**

Set the rejected character aside and roll a replacement. The rejected character remains part of the campaign world and may become a hireling, torchbearer, porter, camp follower, relative, debtor, hanger-on, or other poor bastard attached to the party.

### Ability Score Increases

Every time you gain a level, choose an ability score to try to improve. Roll a d20; if the result is higher than the current score, increase that ability by 1.

You may make two attempts at each level-up, but you can improve only one ability score in this way.

## Luck & Combat

### Fleeting Luck

Each character starts the session with **1 Fleeting Luck**. Additional Fleeting Luck may be awarded for critical successes, clever plans, bold risks, excellent problem-solving, memorable roleplay, or actions that make the table more fun.

A point of Fleeting Luck may be spent to add **+1 to a roll after the die is rolled**. Multiple points may be spent on the same roll. Fleeting Luck may also be spent to help another player character on a one-for-one basis.

> **Fleeting Luck is unstable.** When any player character rolls a natural 1 on an action, all Fleeting Luck held by the party is lost.

### Shields Shall Be Splintered

A character carrying a shield may destroy that shield to completely negate the damage from a single physical attack.

The shield must be ready and plausibly useful against the attack. A shield can stop a sword blow, spear thrust, arrow, thrown axe, monster claw, or similar hit. It cannot stop poison gas, falling damage, mind control, curses, fireballs, or other hazards where a shield would not reasonably help.

After the shield splinters, remove it from the character sheet. The character loses the shield’s AC bonus until another shield is equipped.

**Magic shields may survive splintering.** Roll a saving throw for the item. On a success, the shield absorbs the blow and remains intact. On a failure, it is destroyed.

## Inventory & Encumbrance

### Carrying Capacity

A character has a number of **inventory slots equal to their Strength score or 10, whichever is higher**.

### Inventory Slots

- Most significant carried items occupy **1 inventory slot**.
- Particularly bulky or cumbersome items may occupy **2 or more slots**, as determined by the referee.
- Armor occupies **no inventory slots while worn**.
- Up to **two equipped items** occupy no slots. These can include weapons, shields, wands, torches, or similar objects kept immediately ready.
- Small or worn objects—such as jewelry, cloaks, ordinary clothing, keys, and other negligible items—are typically **slot-free**.
- Quantities of similar items can be bundled into a single slot when reasonable. The referee determines how many constitute one slot.
- The referee determines an item’s slot cost whenever circumstances warrant it.

### Encumbrance & Movement

A character’s **base movement rate is determined by the armor they are wearing**.

| Slots over capacity | Movement penalty |
| ---: | ---: |
| 1–2 | −10′ |
| 3–4 | −20′ |
| 5–6 | −30′ |
| 7–8 | −40′ |

Continue reducing movement by **10′ for every additional 2 slots, or fraction thereof**. The penalty begins as soon as capacity is exceeded.

Movement cannot be reduced below **0′**. At 0′, the character is severely overloaded and can move only at a pace determined by the referee.

### Sacks & Other Containers

- A **small sack** holds up to **2 slots** of items, or approximately 200 coins.
- A **large sack** holds up to **4 slots** of items, or approximately 400 coins.
- An empty sack occupies **1 inventory slot**.
- A loaded sack occupies a number of slots equal to the combined slot cost of its contents. The sack itself is not counted when full.

Whether a loaded sack requires one or both hands is handled through table rulings.

## Exploration & Survival

### Wilderness Travel

**1 hex = 3 miles.** A party has **10 Movement Points (MP) per day**.

| Terrain | MP / Hex |
|---|--:|
| Open / settled | 1 |
| Hills / woods / broken | 2 |
| Severe mountains / badlands | 4 |
| Marsh / swamp | 5 |

**Mounts:** Add **6 MP/day** in terrain where riding is practical. Harsh terrain may negate the bonus, require mounts to be led, or be impassable to them.

Roads cost **1 MP**; trails reduce cost by 1, minimum 1. Searching a hex costs **3 MP**.

**Encounters:** Check **1-in-6 per 3 MP spent traveling or searching**.

**Navigation:** When conditions make reliable wayfinding uncertain, check once per day to become lost: **1-in-6 easy, 2-in-6 difficult, 3-in-6 severe terrain**. Barbarians, huntsmen, rangers, scouts, and relevant backgrounds roll d8 instead. If lost, veer one hex-face left or right.

**Locating Sites:** Chance to find specific features within a hex is **3-in-6 open, 2-in-6 hills/broken, 1-in-6 woods/swamp/severe**. Good clues improve the chance.

Weather, river crossings, forced travel, and unusual conditions modify MP at the referee's discretion.

### Drowning

The base amount of time a character can hold their breath underwater is a number of rounds equal to **one-third of Constitution, rounded up**. This assumes the character can take a deep breath before submerging and does not perform strenuous activity underwater. Normal swimming, diving, surfacing, searching, opening a chest, or otherwise moderate activity is allowed.

If the character could not fill their lungs before going under, halve this time, rounded up. If the character engages in strenuous activity such as combat, halve it again. These penalties are cumulative. A character can always hold their breath for at least one round.

After this limit expires, the character must make a **Constitution test each round** to remain conscious.

On a failed test, the character falls unconscious and cannot move under their own power. If the character is not brought to breathable air before the end of the following round, they drown and die.

*Adapted from the Wilderness Survival Guide.*

## Class Rules

### Clerics & Priests: Spontaneous Cure Casting

Clerics and Priests may sacrifice any **uncast memorized spell** to cast a **Cure X Wounds** spell they know, provided the sacrificed spell is of **equal or higher spell level**.

- The Cure spell must already be among the caster’s **known spells**.
- The Cure spell functions normally, regardless of the level of the spell sacrificed.
- This applies only to **Cure X Wounds** spells and does not allow spontaneous casting of their reversed *Inflict* forms.

### Runegravers: Learning Runes

- Runegravers may know **any number of runes**.
- They may learn any rune encountered in play, provided they can cast runes of that level.
- Daily rune slots remain unchanged.

#### Attuning to a Discovered Rune

To learn a complete, functioning rune from an inscription, artifact, teacher, or similar source:

- Spend **3 days per rune level** in downtime.
- The original rune or object must remain available for study.
- At completion, roll the normal **cleric chance to learn a new spell**.
- Success permanently adds the rune to the Runegraver’s known runes.
- Failure means the Runegraver cannot learn that exemplar again until gaining a level or finding another source or teacher.
- There is no gp cost, and the original rune or object is not consumed.

#### Reconstructing a Rune

Incomplete inscriptions, fragmentary notes, oral accounts, and similar sources require independent spell research:

- **1 week + 500 gp per rune level**.
- Use the normal research and qualification procedure.

#### Persistent Runes

A Runegraver may have only **one active instance of each persistent rune** at a time.

### Thieves: Skill Allocation

Thieves use the normal 1st-level Progressive Thief Abilities, but thereafter allocate their own advancement rather than using Table 16.

- **Levels 2–11:** Gain **4 skill points** per level.
- **Level 12:** Gain **2 skill points**.
- Each point increases one Progressive Thief Ability by **+1-in-12**.
- No more than **2 points per level** may be assigned to the same ability.
- Points must be spent when gained and cannot be banked.
- **Read Scrolls** cannot receive points before **5th level**.
- Exceptional attribute bonuses apply normally and may raise the effective chance above the listed cap.

| Ability | Maximum |
| --- | ---: |
| Climb | 10:12 |
| Decipher Script | 5:12 |
| Discern Noise | 9:12 |
| Hide | 10:12 |
| Manipulate Traps | 8:12 |
| Move Silently | 10:12 |
| Open Locks | 8:12 |
| Pick Pockets | 9:12 |
| Read Scrolls | 5:12 |

This provides **42 advancement points**, equal to the total improvement of a standard Hyperborea thief from 1st through 12th level.
